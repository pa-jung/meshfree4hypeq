### Numerical Experiment: Linear Advection of a Discontinuity

To assess the fundamental properties of the implemented schemes, such as numerical diffusion, dispersion, and the effectiveness of the MOOD framework, we first consider the one-dimensional linear advection equation:
$$
u_t + a u_x = 0
$$
This is the simplest hyperbolic conservation law and serves as an essential benchmark. Any robust numerical method must be able to solve this problem accurately before being applied to more complex nonlinear systems.

#### Experimental Setup

We solve the equation with an advection speed of $a=0.5$, as specified by the `PDE_params`. The initial condition is a Riemann problem, specifically a step function (or contact discontinuity), defined by:
$$
u(x, 0) = 
\begin{cases} 
1.0 & \text{if } x \le x_0 \\
0.0 & \text{if } x > x_0 
\end{cases}
$$
The exact initial position $x_0$ is chosen such that after advecting for the specified `tmax`, the discontinuity is centered in the plot for clear visualization. For this test, a uniform grid (`randomness_factor = 0.0`) is used, discretized with `N` = 200 particles on the domain `xmin` = -5.0 to `xmax` = 10.0. Using a regular grid removes any errors associated with grid irregularity and allows for a direct assessment of each scheme's fundamental properties when handling a sharp discontinuity.

#### Rationale for Method Selection

This experiment is designed to compare the performance of several methods under ideal grid conditions when faced with a sharp, non-smooth feature:
1.  **Classical Schemes (`LW`, `LWMOOD`):** The second-order Lax-Wendroff scheme (`LW`) is included as a classical high-order benchmark. It is known to be highly dispersive and produce severe oscillations at discontinuities. Its MOOD-stabilized counterpart, `LWMOOD` (which uses a diffusive first-order fallback), serves to demonstrate the baseline effect of stabilization in a finite volume context.
2.  **High-Order Meshfree Schemes (`RK2MUSCL2`, `RK4MUSCL5MOOD`):** The core of the investigation lies in the meshfree methods. We test a second-order MUSCL reconstruction with a second-order Runge-Kutta timestepper (`RK2MUSCL2`) to observe its raw, unlimited behavior. We also test a very high-order combination (`RK4MUSCL5MOOD`) to see if increasing the order of both the spatial and temporal discretization provides benefits for resolving sharp fronts.
3.  **MOOD-Stabilized Meshfree Schemes:** Several variants of `RK2MUSCL2` with MOOD enabled are tested. This is the most important comparison, as it shows how the detection-and-correction strategy handles the oscillations generated by the high-order meshfree reconstruction.

#### Observations

The figure shows the numerical solution at a fixed final time, compared against the exact analytic solution (a perfectly sharp, advected step function, shown in red).

* **Analytic Solution (Red):** The exact solution is a contact discontinuity that has advected from its initial position to approximately $x=7.0$.
* **Unstabilized High-Order Methods (`LW`, `RK2MUSCL2`):** As expected, both the classical Lax-Wendroff (green dashed line) and the unlimited meshfree `RK2MUSCL2` (purple dash-dot line) fail catastrophically. They produce large, non-physical oscillations (Gibbs phenomenon) on both sides of the discontinuity, rendering the solution useless. This confirms that high-order linear reconstructions are inherently unsuitable for sharp gradients without a limiting or stabilization mechanism.
* **Classical MOOD (`LWMOOD`):** The orange dotted line shows that the MOOD framework successfully stabilizes the Lax-Wendroff scheme. The oscillations are completely eliminated. However, the price is extremely high numerical diffusion, resulting in a very smeared profile that barely resembles the sharp front. This is characteristic of falling back to a first-order accurate method (like Lax-Friedrichs or upwind) at the discontinuity.
* **Meshfree MOOD Schemes (Dotted/Dashed lines):** All variants of the `RK2MUSCL2MOOD` scheme (cyan, yellow, grey) perform exceptionally well. They completely suppress the instabilities while being significantly less diffusive than `LWMOOD`. They capture the shock front over a very small number of particles.
* **High-Order Meshfree MOOD (`RK4MUSCL5MOOD`):** The dark blue dashed line, representing the highest-order method, produces the sharpest profile of all numerical schemes. It resolves the discontinuity over the fewest points. However, it also exhibits the most noticeable overshoot and undershoot immediately adjacent to the jump, a common trade-off when applying very high-order methods to non-smooth solutions.

#### Analysis and Conclusion

This experiment clearly demonstrates the power and necessity of the MOOD framework for stabilizing high-order methods when solving problems with discontinuities. The primary conclusion is that the **`MUSCL+MOOD` approach provides a superior balance between stability and accuracy compared to classical finite volume methods** for this problem. While `LWMOOD` achieves stability, it does so at the cost of excessive diffusion. In contrast, the meshfree `RK2MUSCL2MOOD` schemes remain stable while capturing a sharp front, highlighting the benefit of using a high-order method in smooth regions and only applying the dissipative fallback where absolutely necessary.

Furthermore, the comparison between the `RK2MUSCL2MOOD` and `RK4MUSCL5MOOD` results illustrates the classic trade-off at the high-order frontier: increasing the order further sharpens the discontinuity but can re-introduce small, localized Gibbs-like oscillations (over/undershoots). The choice of the "best" order depends on whether the application prioritizes a perfectly non-oscillatory solution or the sharpest possible resolution of fronts. Overall, the results validate the meshfree MOOD approach as a robust and accurate method for hyperbolic problems with sharp features.
